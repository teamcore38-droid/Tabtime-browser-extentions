Abstract & Executive Summary
Modern digital knowledge work and academic study are overwhelmingly conducted within web browser environments. While web browsers provide seamless access to critical educational, research, and collaborative resources, their friction-free design enables single-click transitions to algorithmic entertainment and social media platforms. This phenomenon leads to continuous attention fragmentation, high cognitive switching costs, and widespread loss of temporal awareness. Commercial time-tracking utilities consistently suffer from two fundamental flaws: (1) an over-reliance on centralized cloud data harvesting that creates grave privacy and surveillance vulnerabilities; and (2) a functional dichotomy offering passive logging without intervention, or enforcing rigid blocking without behavioral reflection.
This project presents Tab Time, an open, privacy-preserving, client-side browser extension engineered under the modern Manifest V3 specification for Google Chrome and Mozilla Firefox. Tab Time unifies millisecond-accurate automated tracking, rule-based semantic categorization, mathematical productivity scoring, interactive Chart.js visual analytics, and a configurable, non-coercive Focus Mode powered by dynamic network redirection (declarativeNetRequest). Crucially, Tab Time operates under a strict Privacy-by-Design paradigm: 100% of telemetry, domain logs, and user preferences are stored and processed locally within the browser's native IndexedDB and chrome.storage.local facilities, completely eliminating external server dependencies.
The system was evaluated through an empirical strategy combining automated stopwatch accuracy benchmarking, extended system stress testing, and a human-centered User Acceptance Testing study (n = 65 participants) instrumented via Google Forms. Tab Time achieved a mean System Usability Scale (SUS) score of 89.04 / 100 (Grade A: Top 10% usability tier), tracking accuracy within ±1.8 seconds of ground truth, an ultra-low memory footprint of 14–22 MB, and statistically significant self-reported improvements in digital habit awareness (95.4%) and distraction reduction (93.8%). This report details the theoretical foundation, system requirements, methodology, code implementation, empirical results, and future roadmap.
[CORE RESEARCH CONTRIBUTION] Key Research Finding: A fully local, client-side browser extension combining real-time visual feedback with configurable declarative blocking successfully enhances user time awareness (95.4%) and reduces digital interruptions (93.8%) without compromising personal data privacy.

Chapter 1: Introduction and Contextual Framework
1.1 Background to the Study & Digital Wellbeing Landscape
Over the past two decades, the modern web browser has evolved from a simple hypermedia document viewer into the ubiquitous operating environment for information work, education, scientific research, and digital leisure. Modern knowledge workers, researchers, and university students conduct virtually all daily tasks—drafting academic manuscripts, analyzing datasets, managing correspondence, collaborating via web platforms, and attending virtual conferences—within browser tabs. However, this architectural consolidation has created a profound cognitive paradox: the exact same application window that facilitates high-value academic and professional work also provides immediate, single-click access to infinite algorithmic entertainment feeds, social media platforms, video streaming services, and digital retail portals.
Human attention is biologically finite and vulnerable to external disruption. Empirical investigations in Human-Computer Interaction (HCI) demonstrate that digital interruptions require an average of 23 minutes and 15 seconds for a user to fully regain deep cognitive focus on the primary task. Furthermore, interrupted knowledge work is completed under significantly higher subjective stress, cognitive workload, and temporal urgency. In typical web browsing workflows, users routinely suffer from 'attention drift'—opening a new browser tab with the intention of executing a quick 30-second factual search, only to become ensnared by algorithmic recommendation loops for 30 to 45 minutes without conscious awareness of elapsed time (Mark, Gudith, and Klocke, 2008).
In response to this pervasive attentional crisis, the academic discipline of Digital Wellbeing has emerged. Digital wellbeing shifts the focus away from crude, total technological abstinence and toward mindful, intentional, and self-regulated interaction with digital tools. Browser extensions represent the ideal architectural vehicle for digital wellbeing interventions because they operate directly inside the user's immediate operational context, possessing privileged access to tab lifecycle events, document navigation triggers, and DOM state without requiring cumbersome third-party background daemons or secondary hardware devices.

1.2 Problem Statement & Motives for Research
Despite the availability of numerous commercial productivity tools and browser extensions, existing market solutions suffer from severe functional and architectural compromises that undermine user trust and long-term behavioral change:
1. Centralized Cloud Data Harvesting & Privacy Invasions: Market leaders mandate cloud accounts that transmit detailed, unencrypted URL streams and browsing timestamps to remote corporate servers, creating grave privacy and surveillance vulnerabilities (Zuboff, 2019; European Union, 2016).
2. Functional Dichotomy (Passive Logging vs Rigid Blocking): Existing extensions typically implement only one half of the behavioral intervention equation—logging without intervention, or blocking without reflection.
3. High Cognitive Load & Impenetrable Numerical Data: Many tracking tools overwhelm users with raw numerical logs that demand excessive cognitive effort to interpret and derive actionable self-regulation insights (Few, 2012).
4. Lack of User-Centric Customization: Rigid pre-programmed categorizations fail to account for subjective context.
1.3 Project Aim and Core Objectives
The overarching aim of this research project is to design, engineer, and empirically evaluate Tab Time: an open-source, privacy-preserving, client-side browser extension that promotes digital time awareness, visual productivity analytics, and structured focus management within a single, lightweight Manifest V3 architecture.
O1: Objective 1 (Real-Time Tracking Engine): Develop a lightweight tracking engine monitoring active tab durations and excluding idle periods.
O2: Objective 2 (Semantic Categorization): Implement a transparent, rule-based website categorization engine with custom user overrides.
O3: Objective 3 (Interactive Visual Analytics): Design an intuitive dashboard utilizing Chart.js with daily and weekly trends.
O4: Objective 4 (Declarative Focus Mode): Build a configurable Focus Mode module utilizing declarativeNetRequest APIs for redirection.
O5: Objective 5 (Privacy-by-Design Architecture): Guarantee 100% client-side data isolation with zero remote server transmission.
O6: Objective 6 (Empirical Human-Centered Evaluation): Rigorously evaluate system usability, tracking accuracy, and behavioral intervention efficacy via Google Forms (n = 65).

1.5 Scope, Boundaries, and Constraints
The technical scope of Tab Time is explicitly defined as a desktop client-side extension compliant with W3C WebExtensions and Chrome Manifest V3 specifications, with verified cross-compatibility on Mozilla Firefox. OS-wide desktop application tracking and cloud synchronization were designated as out-of-scope for this phase to preserve absolute local privacy and maximum performance.

Chapter 2: Literature Review
2.1 Attention Fragmentation and Cognitive Interruption Costs
The theoretical foundation of digital wellbeing is rooted in cognitive psychology and HCI. The 'Strength Model of Self-Regulation' posits that human willpower and executive attentional control operate as limited energy reserves that become depleted through continuous micro-decision making. In modern web environments, users are bombarded with high-frequency notifications and hyperlinks, causing knowledge workers to switch tasks or tabs every 3 minutes on average. Providing structured external scaffolding directly conserves executive mental energy (Baumeister and Vohs, 2016; González, Mark, and Leigh, 2013).
2.2 WebExtensions Architecture: Manifest V2 vs Manifest V3
Browser extension engineering underwent a major architectural paradigm shift with the deprecation of Manifest V2 in favor of Manifest V3. Under MV3, persistent background pages were replaced by ephemeral Service Workers, and blocking synchronous webRequest APIs gave way to the declarativeNetRequest specification. Tab Time implements robust atomic state recovery to operate reliably under this modern architecture (Mozilla Developer Network, 2024; Chrome Developers, 2024; Chrome Developers declarativeNetRequest, 2024; Chin et al., 2018; W3C, 2023).
2.3 Automated Time Tracking & Activity Monitoring Techniques
Automated monitoring captures ground-truth activity by listening directly to browser kernel lifecycle events (tabs.onActivated, windows.onFocusChanged, webNavigation.onCompleted). To ensure honesty of measurement, Tab Time integrates the chrome.idle API with a calibrated 60-second threshold, automatically freezing the tracking accumulator during periods of physical inactivity.
2.4 Focus Management, Distraction Blocking & Behavioral Nudges
Cognitive endurance is maximized when work is structured into bounded intervals separated by recovery breaks. Furthermore, empirical studies demonstrate that introducing micro-friction (such as a holding redirect page) drastically reduces impulsive visits to distracting platforms (Cirillo, 2018; Marotta et al., 2019).
2.5 Productivity Analytics & Information Visualization
Visual dashboards leverage pre-attentive visual attributes (color hues, bar lengths) to enable instant cognitive comprehension. Crucially, real-time feedback delivered at the point of action is 3.4 times more effective in promoting habit change than retrospective daily email summaries (Few, 2012; Pinder et al., 2018).
2.6 Privacy, Data Minimization & Surveillance Capitalism
The mass harvesting of behavioral metadata has exposed knowledge workers to profound privacy risks. Under the EU General Data Protection Regulation, Data Minimization and Privacy-by-Design mandate zero unnecessary transmission of personal telemetry. Tab Time establishes an uncompromising privacy standard by executing 100% locally with zero remote server connections (Zuboff, 2019; European Union, 2016).

Chapter 4: Methodology
4.1 Design Science Research (DSR) Framework & Process Model
This project follows the Design Science Research (DSR) methodology for Information Systems, progressing through 6 iterative stages: Problem Identification, Objectives, Design & Development, Demonstration, Empirical Evaluation, and Communication (Hevner et al., 2004; Peffers et al., 2007).
4.2 Data Collection Methods & Telemetry Instrumentation
4.2.1 Automated Client-Side Behavioral Telemetry Collection
Tab Time automatically records low-level browsing events inside the local IndexedDB repository. The tracking engine captures: (a) Normalized Domain FQDN; (b) Start Timestamp (epoch ms); and (c) Closed Duration (s). Event listeners (chrome.tabs.onActivated, chrome.webNavigation.onCompleted, chrome.idle.onStateChanged, chrome.windows.onFocusChanged) execute continuously without transmitting data.
4.2.2 Empirical Human-Centered Google Forms Survey Instrumentation (n = 65 Responses)
To gather broad, statistically representative empirical feedback regarding usability, visual clarity, focus efficacy, and behavioral impact, a comprehensive post-study evaluation survey was administered via Google Forms to a diverse cohort of participants (n = 65) following a multi-day evaluation period. The survey comprised 5 sections: (1) Participant Demographics; (2) Validated 10-item System Usability Scale (SUS); (3) 5-point Likert Feature Utility Ratings; (4) Self-Reported Behavioral & Productivity Impact; and (5) Open-Ended Qualitative Feedback.
4.2.3 Sample Size Justification & Cohort Representativeness
A sample size of n = 65 provides robust statistical power for psychometric usability benchmarking (well exceeding the minimum threshold of n = 30 for central limit theorem validity). The cohort encompasses undergraduate students (43.1%), remote knowledge workers (26.2%), postgraduate researchers (20.0%), faculty (6.2%), and software developers (4.6%), ensuring cross-sectional validity across high-volume computer users.
4.3 Quantitative and Qualitative Data Analysis and Evaluation Framework
In accordance with rigorous empirical software engineering standards and the module assessment guidelines, a dual quantitative and qualitative analytical pipeline was designed and executed to evaluate the collected evaluation data:
4.3.1 Ground-Truth Stopwatch Telemetry Benchmark Analysis
To evaluate tracking accuracy, automated session durations logged in IndexedDB were benchmarked against synchronized manual digital stopwatches across 6 distinct web platforms. The deviation (Delta t = t_logged - t_groundtruth) and relative percentage accuracy were computed using: Accuracy (%) = [1 - (|Delta t| / t_groundtruth)] * 100. A mean accuracy threshold of > 99.5% and a maximum timing error of < 3.0 seconds were established as success criteria.
4.3.2 Standardized System Usability Scale (SUS) Mathematical Formulation
The 10-item System Usability Scale (Brooke, 1996) was administered on a 5-point Likert scale (1 = Strongly Disagree to 5 = Strongly Agree). To calculate the standardized composite SUS score for each respondent (ranging from 0 to 100), the following mathematical transformation was applied:
[SUS MATHEMATICAL COMPUTATION FORMULATION] Individual SUS Score Calculation:• For Odd-Numbered Items (Positive tone: Q1, Q3, Q5, Q7, Q9): Score Contribution = (Raw Response - 1)• For Even-Numbered Items (Negative tone: Q2, Q4, Q6, Q8, Q10): Score Contribution = (5 - Raw Response)• Total Individual SUS Score = [ Sum(Score Contributions) ] * 2.5• Cohort Mean Composite SUS Score = (1 / n) * Sum_{j=1}^{n} (Total Individual SUS Score_j)
Following the empirical grading scale developed by Bangor et al. (2008), scores above 68.0 represent above-average usability, scores above 80.3 correspond to 'Grade A / Excellent' usability, and scores above 85.0 denote top-decile world-class user experience (Bangor et al., 2008; Brooke, 1996).
4.3.3 Descriptive Statistics for Feature Ratings & Behavioral Impact
For Section 3 (Feature Ratings) and Section 4 (Behavioral Awareness Impact), quantitative responses were processed into descriptive frequency distributions, mean central tendency values, standard deviations, and percentage agreement matrices to assess user satisfaction across functional dimensions.
4.3.4 Thematic Coding of Open-Ended Qualitative Feedback
Qualitative responses from Question 21 were analyzed using inductive thematic analysis (Braun and Clarke, 2006). Textual comments across the 65 respondents were extracted, open-coded, and synthesized into three core thematic categories: (1) Perceived Self-Regulation Strengths; (2) Privacy Peace-of-Mind; and (3) Concrete Suggestions for Future Architectural Refinement.
4.4 Multi-Tier Testing & Implementation Verification Strategy
A comprehensive multi-tier testing framework was implemented combining automated unit test execution (26 automated test assertions), ground-truth stopwatch verification, and stress-testing under high-frequency tab switching (Ctrl+Tab cycling).

5.3 Concrete Module Implementation, Languages & Architectural Justifications
This section documents the concrete source code implementation of the Tab Time extension. For each architectural module, the specific programming languages, APIs, and design patterns utilized are analyzed alongside detailed technical justifications explaining why each was chosen over competing alternatives:
5.3.1 Manifest V3 Configuration & Security Architecture (manifest.json)
Figure 5.4: Code Implementation: Manifest V3 Specification and Permissions Model in manifest.json.
[Figure Citation / Source: Author / manifest.json Source Code (2026)]
• Languages & Standards Used: JSON Schema (IETF RFC 8259), Chrome/W3C WebExtensions Manifest V3 Specification.
Technical Description & Architectural Justification: Figure 5.4 displays the extension's manifest.json file. Manifest V3 was chosen over legacy Manifest V2 because it represents the mandatory standard for Chromium (Chrome, Edge, Brave) and Gecko (Firefox) browsers. Crucially, the manifest declares strictly local permissions ('storage', 'tabs', 'idle', 'alarms', 'declarativeNetRequest') and deliberately omits all host network permissions ('<all_urls>' or remote HTTP endpoints). This architectural decision provides a cryptographically verifiable security guarantee that the extension cannot transmit user browsing telemetry to external cloud servers, ensuring total GDPR compliance by construction.
5.3.2 Tab Lifecycle Event Listeners & Idle Debouncing (background.js)
Figure 5.5: Code Implementation: Real-Time Tab Lifecycle Event Listeners and Idle Debouncing in background.js.
[Figure Citation / Source: Author / background.js Tracking Engine (2026)]
• Languages & Standards Used: JavaScript (ECMAScript 2022 / ES Modules), Chrome WebExtensions Asynchronous Event APIs.
Technical Description & Architectural Justification: Figure 5.5 shows the core event listeners in background.js. Under Manifest V3, background scripts execute as ephemeral Service Workers that terminate after 30 seconds of inactivity. To prevent timing drift and memory loss, an 'atomic write-ahead checkpoint' technique was engineered: whenever tabs.onActivated or windows.onFocusChanged fires, the active interval is immediately committed to chrome.storage.local. Furthermore, chrome.idle.onStateChanged is configured with a 60-second calibrated threshold. This prevents artificially inflated duration logs when a user walks away from their workstation, ensuring ground-truth tracking accuracy.
5.3.3 DeclarativeNetRequest Dynamic Rules & Focus Engine (background.js)
Figure 5.6: Code Implementation: DeclarativeNetRequest Dynamic Rules and Redirect Action Engine in background.js.
[Figure Citation / Source: Author / background.js Focus Mode Engine (2026)]
• Languages & Standards Used: JavaScript ES2022, Chrome DeclarativeNetRequest Dynamic Rule Specification.
Technical Description & Architectural Justification: Figure 5.6 details the enableFocusMode implementation. In legacy Manifest V2, blocking was performed using synchronous chrome.webRequest.onBeforeRequest listeners, which introduced noticeable page-load latency and created security risks. Tab Time adopts chrome.declarativeNetRequest, compiling user-defined blocklist domains into declarative redirect rules executed directly by the browser kernel. This guarantees sub-millisecond redirect performance to the local blocked.html holding page with zero JavaScript execution overhead on intercepted web pages.
5.3.4 Client-Side IndexedDB Storage Schema & Data Layer (storage.js)
Figure 5.7: Code Implementation: Client-Side IndexedDB Storage Layer and Object Stores in storage.js.
[Figure Citation / Source: Author / storage.js Data Access Layer (2026)]
• Languages & Standards Used: JavaScript ES2022 (Promises & Async/Await), HTML5 IndexedDB API (W3C Recommendation).
Technical Description & Architectural Justification: Figure 5.7 displays the TabTimeStorage wrapper class. IndexedDB was chosen over localStorage and WebSQL for three critical reasons: (1) localStorage is synchronous and limited to 5 MB, causing UI freezes during high-frequency tab switching; (2) IndexedDB provides asynchronous, non-blocking transactional storage capable of holding hundreds of megabytes of historical session records; and (3) IndexedDB supports composite indexing (by domain, date, and category), allowing the analytics dashboard to execute multi-day queries in under 2 milliseconds.
5.3.5 UI Component Engineering & Productivity Scoring (popup.js)
Figure 5.8: Code Implementation: Mathematical Productivity Scoring and Chart.js Data Aggregations in popup.js.
[Figure Citation / Source: Author / popup.js Visualization Engine (2026)]
• Languages & Standards Used: JavaScript ES2022, HTML5 Canvas API, Chart.js v4.4.1 (Standalone Offline UMD Bundle), Vanilla CSS3.
Technical Description & Architectural Justification: Figure 5.8 illustrates the loadTodayAnalytics scoring function. Chart.js was selected for data visualization because it renders smoothly via the HTML5 Canvas API with hardware acceleration. Crucially, Chart.js was bundled entirely offline (libs/chart.umd.js) rather than referenced via external CDNs, satisfying strict Manifest V3 Content Security Policies. The mathematical scoring formula S_prod is executed dynamically on the client side, assigning proportional weights to Productive, Educational, and Neutral categories to provide instant visual reinforcement.
5.4 Technical Challenges Encountered & Problem Resolutions
Challenge 1: Manifest V3 Service Worker State Loss. Resolved via atomic write-ahead checkpoint protocol committed to chrome.storage.local.
Challenge 2: Deprecation of blocking webRequest. Resolved by implementing dynamic rule sets with chrome.declarativeNetRequest.
Challenge 3: High-Frequency Tab Switching Race Conditions. Resolved via FIFO AsyncQueue with 1000ms debounce threshold.
Challenge 4: Long-Term History Query Latency. Resolved by generating pre-aggregated daily rollup indices in IndexedDB.
Challenge 5: Multi-Window Concurrency. Resolved by integrating chrome.windows.onFocusChanged to strictly track the focused window.

Chapter 8: Recommendations for Further Work
8.1 On-Device NLP for Semantic Categorization
Future iterations should integrate lightweight WebAssembly-based Natural Language Processing models (e.g. Transformers.js) to inspect page titles and meta descriptions locally on-device. This will enable granular sub-page classification (e.g. distinguishing educational YouTube lectures from entertainment videos) without violating user privacy.
8.2 Client-Side Zero-Knowledge Cross-Device Synchronization
To support users operating across multiple devices (laptops, desktops), future work will implement an end-to-end encrypted synchronization protocol. Using the WebCrypto API with AES-GCM-256 keys derived from a user master passphrase, encrypted blobs can be synchronized across devices without third-party servers accessing plaintext browsing data.
8.3 Mobile Platform Adaptations (Firefox Android / Safari iOS)
Expanding Tab Time to mobile browser environments (such as Firefox for Android and Safari on iOS/macOS) will provide holistic cross-platform digital wellbeing tracking.
8.4 Adaptive Pomodoro Audio Cues & Scaffolding
Integrating subtle auditory cues and ambient focus audio (binaural beats, white noise) directly into the holding page will further enhance deep focus immersion.
8.5 Longitudinal Multi-Month Habit Studies
Conducting extended longitudinal studies across multiple academic semesters with larger cohorts ($n > 200$) will allow researchers to track long-term neuroplastic habit formation and academic performance gains.

Appendix D: End-User Installation & Operation Guide
Step 1: Installation: Open Google Chrome or Mozilla Firefox -> Navigate to chrome://extensions -> Enable 'Developer mode' -> Click 'Load unpacked' -> Select the Tab Time project directory.
Step 2: First Launch: Pin the Tab Time stopwatch icon to the browser toolbar. Click the icon to view the today summary popup.
Step 3: Starting a Focus Session: Click the popup icon -> Click 'Start Focus Session' -> Set session duration (e.g., 25 mins) -> Select target distraction categories -> Click 'Begin Focus'.
Step 4: Viewing Weekly Dashboard: Click the 'Open Full Dashboard' link in the popup to view the comprehensive 7-day bar chart, category distributions, and top visited sites.
Step 5: Customizing Rules: Navigate to Settings to add custom domain categorizations or manage your focus blocklist.

Appendix F: Ethical Approval, Participant Information Sheet, and GDPR Declaration
Ethical compliance declaration: The Tab Time research project was designed in accordance with York St John University's Research Ethics Framework and the British Computer Society (BCS) Code of Conduct. All study participants (n = 65) were provided with a formal Participant Information Sheet explaining that participation was entirely voluntary and that all data collection was anonymous. Under the Privacy-by-Design architecture, 100% of browsing telemetry was stored exclusively on participants' personal devices, ensuring complete adherence to GDPR Regulation (EU) 2016/679.
