Chapter 1: Introduction & Contextual Framework

1.1 Background to the Study

In recent years, the rapid growth of digital technologies has profoundly transformed the way individuals interact with information and entertainment. Web browsers have become an integral part of daily life, serving as gateways to work, social interaction, and leisure activities. While these technologies offer unprecedented convenience, they have also contributed to increasing levels of digital distraction and reduced focus among users. The modern workforce, in particular, faces challenges in maintaining productivity due to the constant influx of notifications, social media, and non-essential browsing activities.

Digital wellbeing has emerged as a critical area of concern, focusing on how technology affects mental health, productivity, and lifestyle habits. Researchers and practitioners alike have explored strategies to mitigate excessive screen time, including time tracking tools, productivity analytics, and intervention mechanisms designed to promote mindful digital engagement. Browser extensions, as lightweight, client-side applications, provide a unique opportunity to implement such interventions directly within users’ browsing environments, offering real-time feedback and actionable insights without requiring additional devices or complex setups.

This project, titled Tab Time: A Browser Extension for Time Awareness, Focus Management, and Digital Wellbeing, is positioned at the intersection of Human-Computer Interaction (HCI), software engineering, and behavioural intervention strategies. The aim is to create a system that not only monitors users' online behaviour but also provides structured interventions that help users regain control over their digital habits. By integrating automated time tracking, visual analytics, and focus management tools, Tab Time aspires to facilitate improved productivity and self-regulated digital engagement among users.

1.2 Problem Statement

The increasing use of web browsers for education, work, communication, and entertainment has led to higher levels of digital distraction and reduced productivity, as users are frequently interrupted by social media, streaming platforms, and other non-essential websites, which negatively affects focus and time management (Mark, Gudith and Klocke, 2008). Although productivity tools and browser extensions are available, many of them only provide basic features such as simple time tracking or website blocking without offering deeper insights into user behaviour. In addition, privacy and security concerns remain a key issue, as many existing tools rely on cloud-based systems that collect and process user data externally, raising trust-related concerns (Zuboff, 2019). Therefore, there is a need for a privacy-focused browser extension that integrates time tracking, productivity analytics, and distraction management in a single system, which is the goal of the proposed Tab Time system.

1.3 Aim and Objectives

Main Aim

The main aim of this project is to design, develop, and evaluate a browser extension that promotes time awareness, focus management, and digital wellbeing by monitoring users’ browsing behaviour and providing real-time productivity insights and distraction management tools.

Objectives

To achieve the overall aim of the project, the following objectives have been identified:

To develop a browser extension capable of accurately tracking the amount of time users spend across different websites and browser tabs in real-time.

To implement a website categorisation system that classifies browsing activities into productive and non-productive categories to improve user awareness of digital habits.

To design and develop an interactive dashboard that visually presents browsing statistics, productivity trends, and usage patterns through charts and graphical analytics.

To implement a focus management module that allows users to create focus sessions and temporarily block distracting websites during work or study periods.

To ensure that all browsing data is processed and stored locally on the user’s device in order to maintain user privacy and data security.

To evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed system in improving users’ awareness of browsing behaviour and reducing digital distractions through system testing and user feedback.

1.4 Research Question and Hypothesis

Research Question

The research conducted in this project is guided by the following central question:

Can a browser extension that combines automated time tracking, visual productivity analytics, and focus management tools improve users’ awareness of their digital habits and reduce online distractions?

This research question focuses on understanding whether behavioural intervention techniques integrated within a browser environment can positively influence productivity and digital wellbeing. The study also investigates how real-time feedback and browsing analytics can assist users in making more informed decisions regarding their online activities.

Hypotheses

To evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed system, the following hypotheses have been established:

Hypothesis 1: Users who actively monitor their browsing behaviour through automated time tracking and visual analytics will demonstrate improved awareness of their digital habits and better time management.

Hypothesis 2: Implementing focus sessions with website blocking functionality will reduce distraction-related interruptions during work and study activities.

Hypothesis 3: Visual representations of browsing statistics, such as charts and productivity summaries, will provide more actionable behavioural insights compared to raw numerical data alone.

The hypotheses will be evaluated through system testing, usage analysis, and user feedback collected during the evaluation phase of the project.

1.5 Scope and Limitations

The scope of this project is focused on the design and development of a browser extension named Tab Time, which aims to enhance users’ awareness of their digital behaviour and improve productivity through time tracking, visual analytics, and focus management features. The system is intended to operate as a client-side extension compatible with modern web browsers such as Google Chrome and Mozilla Firefox. It will monitor user interactions within the browser environment, categorise website usage, and present meaningful insights through an interactive dashboard. Additionally, the system includes a focus mode feature that allows users to temporarily restrict access to selected distracting websites during defined work or study sessions.

The project is primarily limited to browser-level activity tracking and does not extend to monitoring system-wide application usage or mobile device behaviour. As the system is designed to function entirely on the client side, its capabilities are restricted by browser security policies and available WebExtension APIs. Furthermore, the accuracy of productivity classification depends on predefined website categorisation rules, which may not always fully reflect individual user intent or context. Another limitation is that the system does not incorporate advanced machine learning-based behavioural prediction models due to time and resource constraints within the scope of this academic project. Additionally, while the extension is designed with privacy considerations in mind by storing data locally, long-term scalability features such as cloud synchronisation or multi-device tracking are not included in the current implementation.

Chapter 2: Literature Review

2.1 Introduction

The rapid expansion of internet usage and browser-based activities has significantly changed how individuals manage work, study, and leisure, often leading to reduced attention span and productivity due to frequent interruptions and task switching (Mark, Gudith and Klocke, 2008). In response, various digital wellbeing and productivity tools such as time tracking applications, focus management systems, and browser extensions have been developed to support users in managing their online behaviour. However, many existing solutions tend to focus on either passive monitoring or strict website blocking, with limited integration of behavioural analytics and intervention features within a single system. This literature review therefore examines relevant research and technologies in digital wellbeing, browser extension development, and behavioural intervention strategies, while identifying existing gaps that justify the need for the proposed Tab Time system.

2.2 Evolution of Digital Wellbeing and Productivity Tools

The concept of digital wellbeing has evolved alongside the rapid advancement of personal computing and internet technologies. Early productivity tools primarily focused on task management and scheduling, such as desktop-based planners and reminder systems. These tools aimed to improve personal efficiency but did not address the impact of digital distractions or online behaviour patterns. As internet usage became more widespread, the need for tools that could monitor and manage online activity began to emerge.

With the rise of web browsers as central platforms for work and communication, productivity solutions shifted towards browser-based extensions and applications. Tools such as RescueTime and StayFocusd introduced automated time tracking and website blocking features, allowing users to gain insights into their browsing habits and restrict access to distracting websites during focused work sessions. These developments marked a transition from simple productivity tracking to behavioural control mechanisms designed to reduce digital interruptions (González et al., 2013).

Despite these advancements, many existing tools still operate in isolation, focusing either on tracking, blocking, or reporting, but rarely combining all three aspects into a unified system. This limitation highlights the need for more integrated solutions that can provide real-time feedback, actionable insights, and behavioural interventions within a single platform. The proposed Tab Time system is designed to address this gap by combining time tracking, productivity analytics, and focus management into a cohesive browser-based environment.

2.3 Browser Extension Technologies and WebExtensions APIs

Browser extensions are lightweight software components that extend the functionality of web browsers by interacting with web pages and browser-level services. They are typically developed using standard web technologies such as HTML, CSS, and JavaScript, and operate within a controlled browser environment. The introduction of the WebExtensions API has standardised extension development across major browsers, including Google Chrome, Mozilla Firefox, and Microsoft Edge, by providing a unified framework for accessing browser functionalities such as tab management, storage handling, and web request control. Components such as background scripts, content scripts, and extension pages enable a modular architecture where background scripts handle continuous processes, while content scripts interact directly with web pages to capture user activity (Mozilla Developer Network, 2024).

Although browser extension APIs provide powerful capabilities for building feature-rich applications, they are constrained by strict security and performance policies. Extensions must request explicit permissions to access browser data, ensuring user privacy and controlled access to sensitive information. Additionally, continuous background processing can impact browser performance if not carefully managed. These limitations are particularly relevant for systems like Tab Time, which rely on real-time activity tracking and must therefore balance functionality, efficiency, and privacy within the browser environment.

2.4 Time Tracking and Productivity Analytics Systems

Time tracking and productivity analytics systems are designed to help users understand how time is distributed across different tasks and digital activities by monitoring interactions with applications and websites and converting this data into structured reports. Earlier systems relied on manual time logging, where users recorded their own activities, but modern solutions have shifted towards automated tracking to improve accuracy and reduce user effort. Tools such as RescueTime and Toggl Track enable continuous monitoring of digital behaviour and categorise usage into productive and unproductive activities. Research indicates that self-monitoring combined with feedback mechanisms such as dashboards can improve self-regulation and productivity (Baumeister and Vohs, 2016). In addition, the use of data visualisation techniques such as charts and graphs enhances user understanding, as visual representations are generally more effective than raw numerical data in supporting decision-making (Few, 2012).

Despite these advancements, existing productivity analytics systems still have limitations. Many tools provide only retrospective insights, meaning users review their behaviour after it has occurred rather than receiving real-time feedback. Additionally, website categorisation systems are often rigid and may not accurately reflect different user contexts. These limitations highlight the need for more adaptive and real-time solutions that integrate tracking, analytics, and behavioural intervention within a single unified framework, as proposed in the Tab Time system

2.5 Focus Mode and Distraction Blocking Systems

Focus management systems and distraction blocking tools are designed to reduce interruptions during work or study sessions by restricting access to selected websites or applications. These systems are based on behavioural control principles, where external constraints support self-regulation and help users maintain concentration in environments filled with digital distractions. A common approach is website blocking, where specific URLs or categories are restricted for a set duration. Tools such as StayFocusd and Freedom allow users to create custom blocklists and schedule focus sessions, and research shows that reducing access to distracting content can improve sustained attention and reduce task switching (Marotta et al., 2019). More advanced systems introduce “focus modes” that combine timers, usage goals, and real-time enforcement, with studies suggesting that structured time intervals like the Pomodoro technique can enhance cognitive performance and reduce mental fatigue (Cirillo, 2018).

However, existing focus management tools also have limitations. Strict blocking mechanisms can sometimes lead to user frustration or circumvention, reducing their long-term effectiveness. In addition, many tools operate independently and provide limited post-session feedback, meaning users are unable to evaluate the impact of their focus behaviour over time. This lack of reflective insight reduces opportunities for long-term behavioural improvement. These gaps highlight the need for systems that integrate enforcement with analytics, enabling users not only to block distractions but also to understand their productivity outcomes, which is addressed in the proposed Tab Time system.

2.6 Privacy Challenges in Monitoring Software

Monitoring software, including browser-based tracking and productivity tools, raises significant privacy and ethical considerations. These systems inherently collect sensitive information about user behaviour, including website visits, time spent on different platforms, and potentially personal or confidential data. The main challenge lies in balancing the benefits of behavioural insights with the protection of user privacy and compliance with data protection regulations.

Studies have highlighted that users are often concerned about how their browsing data is stored, processed, and potentially shared, particularly with cloud-based analytics tools (Zuboff, 2019). Mismanagement of this data can lead to breaches of trust, identity exposure, and misuse of personal information. Therefore, modern monitoring software must incorporate privacy-by-design principles, such as client-side data processing, anonymization, clear consent mechanisms, and strict data retention policies.

Browser extensions face additional privacy constraints due to security policies enforced by browser vendors. Permissions must be explicitly requested, and access to sensitive APIs is limited to prevent unauthorized data collection. Researchers argue that extensions collecting browsing data should avoid transmitting raw activity data to external servers unless strictly necessary and explicitly consented to by the user (Chin et al., 2018).

The integration of privacy-aware designs with productivity and focus management features is critical for user trust. For tools like Tab Time, processing all tracking and analytics locally on the user’s device ensures that users retain control over their data while still benefiting from insights into their digital habits. Incorporating transparent privacy dashboards and easy-to-understand consent flows further strengthens ethical adherence and adoption.

2.7 Research Gap

Despite the availability of various productivity tracking tools, focus management applications, and browser-based monitoring extensions, there is still a clear gap in the integration of these functionalities into a single unified system. Most existing solutions focus on isolated features such as time tracking, website blocking, or productivity reporting, which limits their ability to provide a comprehensive digital wellbeing experience. Another limitation is the lack of real-time behavioural feedback, as many systems rely on retrospective analysis where users review their activity after it has occurred. Research indicates that real-time feedback is more effective in supporting self-regulation and habit formation compared to delayed reporting approaches (Pinder et al., 2018).

In addition, many existing systems use rigid website categorisation methods that do not consider contextual differences in user intent, leading to less accurate productivity assessments. Privacy concerns also remain significant, particularly in cloud-based solutions that require external data processing and storage, which can reduce user trust (Zuboff, 2019). These limitations highlight the need for a more integrated and privacy-focused solution, and the proposed Tab Time system addresses this gap by combining real-time tracking, visual analytics, and focus management within a single browser-based extension.

3.5 System Architecture and Data Flow

The Tab Time browser extension follows a modular client-side architecture designed to ensure separation of concerns, maintainability, and efficient real-time processing. The system is structured into distinct layers that handle data collection, processing, storage, and presentation within the browser environment. This architecture is essential for supporting continuous time tracking and behavioural analytics without affecting browser performance.

3.5.1 System Architecture Overview

The system is divided into three main layers:

Presentation Layer:This layer includes the user interface components such as the dashboard, popup window, settings page, and focus mode interface. It is responsible for displaying analytics, charts, and user controls in a clear and interactive manner.

Logic Layer (Business Layer):This layer handles the core functionality of the system, including time tracking calculations, website categorisation, productivity scoring, and focus session management. It processes raw data received from browser events and converts it into meaningful insights.

Data Layer:This layer is responsible for storing all user-related data locally using browser storage mechanisms such as Local Storage and IndexedDB. It ensures persistence of browsing statistics, session history, and user preferences.

3.5.2 Data Flow Description

The data flow within the system follows a continuous cycle of monitoring, processing, and feedback:

When a user opens or switches to a browser tab, the Tabs API captures the active tab information.

The tracking engine records the timestamp and URL of the active website.

The system calculates time spent on each website by measuring active duration between tab switches or idle events.

Collected data is passed to the logic layer, where it is categorised into productivity groups based on predefined rules.

Aggregated data is stored locally in the data layer for persistence and future analysis.

The analytics module processes stored data to generate summaries, charts, and productivity scores.

The processed results are displayed in the dashboard interface, providing users with real-time insights into their browsing behaviour.

During focus sessions, the system intercepts navigation requests and triggers content scripts to block or restrict access to distracting websites.

3.6 Tracking Logic and Analytics Processing

The core functionality of the Tab Time browser extension is built around accurate time tracking and meaningful transformation of raw browsing data into productivity insights. This section explains how user activity is captured, processed, and converted into analytics that support digital wellbeing and behavioural awareness.

3.6.1 Time Tracking Mechanism

The system continuously monitors user activity within the browser using WebExtension APIs, primarily focusing on tab switching, active window detection, and navigation events. When a user opens or switches to a tab, the system records a timestamp and associates it with the current URL.

Time spent on a website is calculated by measuring the difference between the active start time and the moment the user switches to another tab or becomes inactive. This approach ensures near real-time tracking without requiring manual input from the user.

To improve accuracy, the system also considers idle detection. If the user is inactive for a defined threshold period, the tracking session is paused to avoid inflating usage statistics.

3.6.2 Website Categorisation Logic

Each visited website is assigned to a predefined category such as productive, educational, social, or entertainment. This categorisation is based on a rule-based mapping system using domain matching (e.g., social media domains mapped to “social” category).

Users may also customise categories manually, allowing flexibility in defining what is considered productive based on individual needs. This ensures that the system remains adaptable rather than enforcing a rigid classification model.

3.6.3 Productivity Scoring Algorithm

The system generates a productivity score by analysing time distribution across different website categories. Time spent on productive categories contributes positively to the score, while time spent on distracting categories reduces the overall value.

Although the scoring mechanism is rule-based in this implementation, it provides a simplified behavioural indicator that helps users understand their browsing efficiency over time.

3.6.4 Analytics Processing and Aggregation

Raw tracking data is periodically processed using scheduled background tasks. The system aggregates data into daily and weekly summaries, calculating metrics such as:

Total browsing time per day

Time spent per category

Most visited websites

Productivity score trends over time

These aggregated results are stored locally and used to generate visual dashboards using charting libraries. The transformation from raw logs to structured analytics is essential for improving user understanding and supporting behavioural change.

3.7 Ethical, Legal, and Professional Considerations

3.7.1 Privacy and Data Protection

A primary ethical concern in this project is the collection and processing of browsing activity data. Although the system does not transmit data to external servers, it still processes potentially sensitive information locally. To address this, the system follows a privacy-by-design approach, ensuring that all data is stored and processed within the user’s device using browser storage mechanisms.

Users are provided with full control over their data, including the ability to reset tracking history, modify categories, and disable tracking features. This aligns with modern data protection principles that emphasise user autonomy and transparency (European Union, 2016).

3.7.2 User Consent and Transparency

Ethical software development requires that users are fully informed about what data is being collected and how it is used. In the context of Tab Time, users must explicitly enable tracking features before any data collection begins. Clear in-app notifications and settings explanations are provided to ensure transparency.

This approach supports informed consent, which is a key principle in ethical computing and user-centred design practices.

3.7.3 Legal Considerations

From a legal perspective, the system is designed to align with general data protection regulations such as the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), particularly in relation to data minimisation and purpose limitation. Although the system does not operate in a cloud environment, adhering to these principles ensures that the design remains compliant with widely accepted international standards.

Additionally, the system avoids collecting personally identifiable information (PII), focusing only on anonymised behavioural data such as domain names and time intervals.

4.3 User Interface Design

The design follows Human-Computer Interaction (HCI) principles, particularly consistency, minimal cognitive load, and clear visual hierarchy.

4.3.1 Design Principles

The UI design is guided by the following principles:

4.4 Implementation of Core Features

This section explains how the main functionalities of the Tab Time browser extension were implemented in practice. The implementation focuses on translating the designed modules into working features using JavaScript-based browser extension technologies and WebExtension APIs. Each feature was developed in a modular manner to ensure separation of concerns and ease of maintenance.

4.4.1 Time Tracking Implementation

The time tracking feature is implemented using browser tab and navigation event listeners. The system continuously monitors the active tab and records timestamps whenever a user switches between tabs or windows.

The basic logic follows a session-based approach:

When a tab becomes active, a start timestamp is recorded

When the tab changes, the time difference is calculated

The duration is then added to the corresponding website entry

Idle detection is used to pause tracking when no user activity is detected

This ensures that only active engagement time is recorded, improving the accuracy of usage statistics.

4.4.2 Website Categorisation Implementation

Website categorisation is implemented using a rule-based mapping system. Each URL is matched against a predefined set of domain patterns to determine its category.

For example:

Social media domains → Social category

Educational websites → Productive category

Entertainment platforms → Leisure category

Users can also override default categories through a settings interface, allowing personalised classification of websites.

This implementation ensures flexibility while maintaining a structured classification system for analytics generation.

4.4.3 Productivity Scoring Implementation

The productivity score is calculated based on weighted time distribution across categories. Each category is assigned a weight reflecting its productivity impact.

A simplified logic is applied:

Productive categories increase the score

Neutral categories maintain balance

Distracting categories reduce the score

The final score is normalised into a percentage format to make it easily understandable for users.

This approach provides a high-level indicator of user productivity trends over time.

4.4.4 Focus Mode Implementation

Focus Mode is implemented using a combination of blocklists and real-time URL interception. When a focus session is active:

The system checks each navigation request against the blocklist

If a match is found, the request is blocked

A custom notification page is displayed instead

The system also uses a timer mechanism to track session duration and automatically disables blocking once the session ends.

This ensures strict enforcement of focus rules while maintaining user control.

4.4.5 Data Storage Implementation

All user data is stored locally using browser storage APIs. Two storage methods are used:

Local Storage: For lightweight settings such as user preferences and configuration data

IndexedDB: For structured time tracking logs and historical analytics data

Data is periodically updated to ensure persistence and consistency across sessions without relying on external servers.

Furthermore, behavioural improvements were mainly observed in short-term scenarios, indicating the need for longer-term evaluation.
